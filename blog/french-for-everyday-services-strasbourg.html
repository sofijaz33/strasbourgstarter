French for Everyday Services in Strasbourg: What to Say in 10 Real-Life Situations

Published November 2026 · 10 min read

By Sofija Zarkovic, founder of Strasbourg Starter

Most French phrasebooks teach you how to order a coffee. They don't teach you what to say when your coat needs cleaning, your radiator has gone cold, or a pharmacist asks a question you didn't see coming. And with winter arriving, those are exactly the moments you'll meet.

This guide is organised by situation, not by alphabet. For each place you'll find the words and phrases to say, what they mean, and, just as important, what you'll hear back, because the reply is usually the part that throws people. At the end there's a short section that only applies in Strasbourg, and an FAQ.

The one rule for every situation below: start with "Bonjour, madame / monsieur" and end with "Merci, bonne journée". Skipping the greeting is the single most common mistake newcomers make, and it changes how everything after it is received. And always use vous, not tu.

1. At the pressing: how to say "dry cleaning" in French

A pressing is a dry cleaner. A laverie is a self-service laundromat, which is a different thing. Winter coats, suits and anything labelled "dry clean only" go to the pressing.

Say thisIt means
Je voudrais faire nettoyer ce manteau, s'il vous plaît.I'd like this coat cleaned, please.
Il y a une tache de vin / de café / de graisse ici.There's a wine / coffee / grease stain here.
C'est prêt pour quand ?When will it be ready?
Je viens récupérer mes vêtements. Voici mon ticket.I'm here to collect my clothes. Here's my ticket.
Est-ce que vous faites les retouches ? Je voudrais raccourcir ce pantalon.Do you do alterations? I'd like to shorten these trousers.

You'll hear: "C'est à quel nom ?" (What name?) · "Ce sera prêt jeudi." (It'll be ready Thursday.) · "Vous avez votre ticket ?" (Do you have your ticket?)

Keep the ticket. Without it, collecting your clothes becomes a conversation you don't want to have in French.

2. At the pharmacy: what to say when you're ill

Look for the green cross. French pharmacists can advise on minor problems without an appointment, and antibiotics always need a prescription (une ordonnance). In winter, this is where the cold and flu vocabulary earns its keep.

Say thisIt means
J'ai besoin de quelque chose pour le rhume / la toux / le mal de gorge.I need something for a cold / cough / sore throat.
Est-ce que c'est en vente libre ?Is this sold without a prescription?
Je suis allergique à la pénicilline.I'm allergic to penicillin.
Comment dois-je le prendre ? Combien de fois par jour ?How should I take it? How many times a day?
Voici mon ordonnance. Je voudrais le générique.Here's my prescription. I'd like the generic.
Est-ce qu'on peut parler plus discrètement ?Can we speak more privately?

You'll hear: "Vous avez de la fièvre ?" (Do you have a fever?) · "Vous avez une ordonnance ?" · "Vous avez votre carte Vitale ?" · "Vous avez une mutuelle ?" (Do you have supplementary insurance?)

Out of hours? Call 3237 or check 3237.fr to find the on-duty pharmacy (pharmacie de garde) at night, on Sundays and on public holidays. A medical emergency is 15 (SAMU). When you name a medicine, use the active ingredient, such as paracétamol, rather than a brand.

3. At the doctor's: booking and describing symptoms

Most appointments are booked online, but you'll still need to explain things in person. If you're still setting up your health cover, the full process is in our healthcare and insurance guide.

Say thisIt means
Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous avec le docteur.I'd like to make an appointment with the doctor.
Je n'ai pas encore de médecin traitant.I don't have a regular GP yet.
J'ai mal à la gorge / à la tête / au ventre.I have a sore throat / headache / stomach ache.
J'ai de la fièvre depuis deux jours.I've had a fever for two days.
Je tousse beaucoup, surtout la nuit.I'm coughing a lot, especially at night.
Pouvez-vous me faire un certificat médical ?Can you write me a medical certificate?

You'll hear: "Qu'est-ce qui vous amène ?" (What brings you in?) · "Où avez-vous mal ?" (Where does it hurt?) · "Depuis quand ?" (Since when?) · "Vous êtes allergique à quelque chose ?" · "Je vous fais une ordonnance." (I'll write you a prescription.)

4. At La Poste: parcels, registered letters and the dreaded avis de passage

Say thisIt means
Je voudrais envoyer cette lettre en recommandé.I'd like to send this letter registered.
Avec accusé de réception, s'il vous plaît.With proof of delivery, please.
C'est combien pour envoyer ce colis en Allemagne / aux États-Unis ?How much to send this parcel to Germany / the US?
J'ai reçu un avis de passage. Je viens chercher un colis.I got a delivery notice. I'm here to collect a parcel.
Je voudrais des timbres, s'il vous plaît.I'd like some stamps, please.

You'll hear: "Vous avez une pièce d'identité ?" · "Vous voulez un suivi ?" (Do you want tracking?) · "Remplissez ce bordereau." (Fill in this form.)

Don't ignore the avis de passage. A registered letter or Colissimo is held for 15 calendar days from the day after the notice and then goes back to the sender. Bring your ID.

5. At the lab: how to say "blood test" in French

Blood tests aren't done at the doctor's office. You take your prescription to a laboratoire d'analyses, where a nurse or technician takes the sample. Most labs have set opening hours and many accept walk-ins, though some can be booked through Doctolib or by phone, and Saturday afternoons are busy.

Say thisIt means
Je viens faire une prise de sang. Voici mon ordonnance.I'm here for a blood test. Here's my prescription.
Est-ce que je dois être à jeun ?Do I need to be fasting?
Je suis à jeun depuis ce matin.I haven't eaten since this morning.
Je voudrais faire une analyse d'urine.I'd like a urine test.
Je prends des médicaments.I'm taking medication.
J'ai tendance à faire des malaises. Je peux m'allonger ?I tend to feel faint. Can I lie down?
Quand est-ce que j'aurai les résultats ? Je peux les recevoir en ligne ?When will I get the results? Can I receive them online?

You'll hear: "Vous êtes à jeun ?" (Are you fasting?) · "Vous avez votre ordonnance ? Votre carte Vitale ?" · "Serrez le poing." (Make a fist.) · "Vous allez sentir une petite piqûre." (You'll feel a small prick.) · "Gardez le pansement quelques heures." (Keep the dressing on for a few hours.)

Ask about fasting (à jeun) when you book. Results usually come online with a login, or on paper, and normally go to the doctor who prescribed the test too, so book a follow-up appointment to go through them. Most tests can also be done without a prescription, but then they are generally not reimbursed.

6. At the bank: cards, RIB and transfers

Say thisIt means
Je voudrais ouvrir un compte.I'd like to open an account.
Pouvez-vous me donner un RIB ?Can you give me my bank details (IBAN document)?
J'ai perdu ma carte bancaire. Je voudrais faire opposition.I've lost my card. I want to block it.
Je voudrais faire un virement.I'd like to make a transfer.
Ma carte ne marche pas.My card isn't working.

You'll hear: "Vous avez un justificatif de domicile ?" (proof of address) · "Une pièce d'identité, s'il vous plaît." · "Vous avez un avis d'imposition ?" (tax notice)

A RIB (relevé d'identité bancaire) is the one-page document with your IBAN. Employers, landlords and CAF will all ask for it, so keep a PDF on your phone.

7. At the mairie or préfecture: paperwork vocabulary

Say thisIt means
J'ai rendez-vous à dix heures.I have an appointment at ten o'clock.
Je voudrais renouveler mon titre de séjour.I'd like to renew my residence permit.
Quels documents dois-je apporter ?Which documents do I need to bring?
Je viens déposer mon dossier.I'm here to submit my file.
Est-ce que vous pouvez me donner une attestation ?Can you give me a certificate / confirmation letter?

You'll hear: "Il manque une pièce." (A document is missing.) · "Vous avez pris rendez-vous ?" · "Prenez un numéro." (Take a ticket.) · "Revenez avec une photocopie." (Come back with a photocopy.)

Bring originals and photocopies of everything. "Il manque une pièce" is the phrase you will hear most often, and the best defence is a folder with a copy of every document.

8. At the hairdresser's: cut, trim and colour

Say thisIt means
Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous pour une coupe.I'd like to book a haircut.
Juste couper les pointes, s'il vous plaît.Just trim the ends, please.
Pas trop court.Not too short.
Court partout / plus court sur les côtés.Short all over / shorter on the sides.
Je voudrais des mèches / un balayage.I'd like highlights.
Avec une frange.With a fringe (bangs).

You'll hear: "Vous voulez un shampoing ?" · "Comme d'habitude ?" (The usual?) · "Un brushing ?" (a blow-dry) · "Ça vous va ?" (Are you happy with it?)

9. When the heating or water breaks: the winter emergency

This is the one situation where you'll be on the phone, in French, in a hurry. If you rent, tell your landlord or agency first because they decide who pays. More on that in our housing guide.

Say thisIt means
Mon chauffage ne marche pas.My heating isn't working.
Le radiateur reste froid.The radiator stays cold.
Il n'y a plus d'eau chaude.There's no hot water.
Il y a une fuite d'eau.There's a water leak.
Pouvez-vous venir aujourd'hui ? C'est urgent.Can you come today? It's urgent.
Combien coûte le déplacement ? Pouvez-vous m'envoyer un devis ?How much is the call-out fee? Can you send me a quote?

You'll hear: "C'est urgent ?" · "Quelle est votre adresse ? Quel étage ?" · "Quel est le code de l'immeuble ?" (door code) · "Je passe demain entre quatorze et seize heures." (I'll come tomorrow between 2 and 4 pm.)

10. At the garage: servicing and winter tyres

Say thisIt means
Ma voiture ne démarre pas.My car won't start.
Je voudrais faire la révision.I'd like a service.
Il y a un bruit bizarre quand je freine.There's a strange noise when I brake.
Je voudrais passer aux pneus hiver.I'd like to switch to winter tyres.
Pouvez-vous me faire un devis ? C'est prêt quand ?Can you give me a quote? When will it be ready?

You'll hear: "Laissez-moi les clés." · "Il faut commander la pièce." (We need to order the part.) · "Il faut compter deux jours." (Count on two days.) · "C'est la batterie." (It's the battery.)

Driving to the Vosges this winter? Winter-equipment rules apply on some signposted mountain roads. Ask: "Est-ce que j'ai besoin de pneus hiver ou de chaînes pour les Vosges ?" and check the current rules before you go.

Only in Strasbourg: three things you won't need in Lyon

A few words of Alsatian

Alsatian isn't French, and spelling varies from village to village, but these are understood everywhere and always get a smile:

  • Salü — hi, or bye (informal)
  • Merci vielmols — thank you very much
  • A guete! — enjoy your meal
  • Wie geht's? — how are you?

The "régime local" at the pharmacy and the doctor

Employees in Alsace-Moselle are usually covered by a local supplementary scheme, which is why you may be asked about it when you pay. If you're unsure whether it applies to you, ask your employer's HR or the health insurance office: "Est-ce que je suis affilié au régime local d'Alsace-Moselle ?" Staff of EU institutions are usually on their own scheme.

Kehl: where the pressing is called a Reinigung

Many people cross the Rhine to shop. A few words help over there: Apotheke (pharmacy), Drogerie (drugstore) and Reinigung (the German word for the dry cleaner). Don't assume a French prescription works across the border, and ask the pharmacist first.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to speak French to live in Strasbourg?

Not to get started, especially if you work at an international institution, but everyday services such as the pharmacy, the post office, the mairie and tradespeople mostly run in French. Learning a few set phrases for each situation makes daily life much smoother, and people respond warmly to the effort.

What is a pressing in France?

A pressing is a dry cleaner. You drop off clothes and collect them later with a ticket. A laverie (or laverie automatique) is a self-service laundromat, and a teinturerie is another word for a dry cleaner that also dyes fabric.

How do I say "I don't speak French well" politely?

Say: "Je ne parle pas très bien français. Pouvez-vous parler plus lentement, s'il vous plaît ?" It means: I don't speak French very well, could you speak more slowly please? Start with "Bonjour" first.

Should I use tu or vous with shopkeepers and officials?

Use vous with shopkeepers, pharmacists, doctors, bank staff and officials. Tu is for friends, family and children.

Why do I have to say bonjour before asking for anything?

In France, starting a conversation without a greeting is considered abrupt. Say "Bonjour" (or "Bonsoir" in the evening) and add "madame" or "monsieur" before you ask anything, and say "Merci, au revoir" when you leave.

How do I find a pharmacy open at night or on Sunday in Strasbourg?

Call 3237 (the national pharmacie de garde number, charged per minute) or check 3237.fr. The on-duty pharmacies are also listed on the door of any pharmacy. For a medical emergency, call 15.

Do I need a prescription for a blood test in France?

Usually yes if you want it reimbursed: take the ordonnance from your doctor to a laboratoire d'analyses. Many tests can also be done without one, but they are then generally not reimbursed and you pay the full price. Ask whether you need to be à jeun (fasting), and expect results online or on paper.

How long do I have to collect a parcel after an avis de passage?

Registered letters and Colissimo parcels are held at the post office for 15 calendar days from the day after the avis de passage is left. Bring your ID and the notice.

What are the emergency numbers in France?

112 is the European emergency number. In France you can also call 15 for the SAMU (medical emergencies), 17 for the police and 18 for the fire brigade.

Can I use a translation app at the counter?

Yes, but greet people in French first and then show your screen. At the pharmacy, use the name of the active ingredient (for example paracétamol) rather than a brand name, because brands differ between countries.

The short version

Say "Bonjour, madame / monsieur" first and use vous. Learn one or two phrases for each place, but pay more attention to what you'll hear back: "Vous êtes à jeun ?", "Il manque une pièce" and "C'est à quel nom ?" come up constantly. Keep the pharmacy number (3237) and the emergency number (15) saved on your phone before winter.
