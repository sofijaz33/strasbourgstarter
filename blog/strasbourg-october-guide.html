Everything Happening in Strasbourg This October

Published October 2026 · 8 min read

By Sofija Zarkovic, founder of Strasbourg Starter

October in Strasbourg has a split personality. The first half is still harvest season and open-air festivals; the second half, the city quietly empties out for the Toussaint school break while the news fills up with strike notices. If you're new here, both halves can catch you off guard for different reasons. Here's what's actually happening, from October 1 to November 1.

The harvest festivals: Vendanges and the Journées de l'Architecture

Alsace's grape harvest wraps up in October, and Strasbourg marks it properly: the Fête des Vendanges et du Goût takes over Place Gutenberg from 9 to 11 October, with regional wine, food producers and tastings — a much less touristy, more genuinely local event than the Christmas market, and a good one to actually go to if you want to understand Alsace's food culture rather than just its holidays.

Running for most of the month in parallel, the Journées de l'Architecture (1–25 October) opens up buildings and sites across Strasbourg and Alsace with guided visits and talks focused on architecture and urban design — a quieter, less crowded cousin of September's European Heritage Days, worth checking if that one passed you by.

Flea market season: brocantes, braderies and vide-greniers

Autumn is peak season for Strasbourg's flea markets, and there's genuinely something most weekends in October — useful to know whether you're furnishing a first flat cheaply or just want a Sunday-morning wander. Confirmed dates so far this month:

  • 3–4 October — Brocante Place du Corbeau (Saturday) and the Foyer Saint-Arbogast vide-grenier (Sunday)
  • 11 October — Marché aux puces du Port du Rhin, Place de l'Hippodrome
  • 17–18 October — Secours populaire's luxury & vintage clothing sale
  • 25 October — another city-wide vide-grenier weekend

None of these need booking — you just turn up, ideally early if you're hunting for something specific, since the good stuff moves fast. It's also one of the more low-key ways to strike up conversation with actual Strasbourgeois rather than other internationals, if that's something you're working on through the community groups you've already found.

Strikes and protests already on the calendar

Autumn is strike season in France most years, and 2026 is no exception — this year's tension is largely tied to the 2027 budget debate. A few things are already confirmed and worth knowing about, separate from whatever gets called at short notice:

  • A national firefighters' strike has been running since 8 September and continues through 20 October — an ongoing labour dispute rather than a single day of disruption.
  • Two separate national aviation strikes are scheduled: easyJet pilots on 1–2 October, and a broader aviation strike from 17–21 October.
If you're flying anywhere for the Toussaint break, note that the second aviation strike (17–21 October) lands exactly on the first days of the school holiday — precisely when flights are most likely to be full and disruption hits hardest. If you have travel booked in that window, it's worth checking your airline's strike policy and having a backup plan before you're standing in an airport.

Beyond these, expect the usual pattern: public-sector unions have already held one large joint demonstration in Strasbourg this autumn, and further strike days are plausible through October and into November as the budget process continues, typically announced with only a week or two of notice. When they happen, trams and buses usually keep running on a reduced schedule rather than stopping entirely, and it's usually school cafeterias and some public offices that close rather than public transport. Checking CTS's own traffic updates the morning of any announced strike is the most reliable way to know what's actually affected that day, rather than relying on general news.

The Toussaint shutdown: two weeks, one big closure

The single biggest thing to plan around this month: Toussaint school holidays run from Saturday 17 October to Monday 2 November, common to every school zone in France, including Strasbourg's zone. All Saints' Day itself falls on Sunday 1 November, a public holiday.

Two weeks is longer than most newcomers expect for what looks, on paper, like a single-day holiday. In practice: schools close entirely for the fortnight, many administrative offices run on skeleton staff or close outright around the 1st, and a lot of small businesses — especially those run by a single owner — simply shut for part or all of the break. If you have anything time-sensitive with a préfecture, CAF, or similar — anything covered in the admin and banking side of settling in — the useful move is to get it filed before 17 October rather than assume you can squeeze it in during the break.

The other, more low-key tradition worth knowing: All Saints' Day is when French families visit cemeteries and lay chrysanthemums on family graves — which is also why, if you're apartment-hunting or just wandering the city on 1 November, you'll notice flower stalls everywhere selling almost nothing but chrysanthemums for about a week beforehand.

The short version

Go to the Fête des Vendanges (9–11 Oct) if you want an authentic taste of Alsace. Check the flea market dates if you're furnishing a flat. If you're flying during the Toussaint break, watch the 17–21 October aviation strike closely. And get anything administrative filed before 17 October — the two-week shutdown catches almost every newcomer's first year.
