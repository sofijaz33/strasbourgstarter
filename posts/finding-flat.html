How to open a bank account in France as a foreigner

June 2025 · 6 min read

Opening a bank account is one of the first things you need to do when you arrive in Strasbourg — you'll need it for your rental contract, your employer, and to receive CAF housing benefits. The good news: it's very doable, even without a French social security number yet.

Which bank should you choose?

Not all French banks are equally welcoming to newly arrived foreigners. Here are the most practical options:

  • Boursorama / Hello Bank / Fortuneo — Online banks, fast to open, very foreigner-friendly. No fees. Best choice if you can wait a week for the card.
  • La Banque Postale — Available at any post office, legally required to open an account for anyone legally residing in France (this is called the droit au compte). Slower but a guaranteed fallback.
  • BNP Paribas / Société Générale / Crédit Mutuel — Traditional banks. Usually require more documents and an appointment, but offer in-person support which can be helpful.
💡 Tip: If you're in a hurry, open an online account first (Boursorama takes about 5–7 days), then open a traditional account later if needed for your employer.

Documents you'll need

Requirements vary slightly between banks, but most will ask for:

  • Valid passport or EU ID card
  • Proof of address in France (a rental contract, EDF bill, or even an attestation from your host)
  • Proof of income or employment (work contract, payslip, or student enrollment certificate)
  • Your visa or residence permit if you're a non-EU citizen

If you've just arrived and don't have a French address yet, ask your employer or university for a attestation d'hébergement — a letter confirming you are staying at their address. Banks usually accept this for the first few weeks.

What if a bank refuses you?

Under French law, every resident has the right to a basic bank account. If a bank refuses, you can invoke the droit au compte procedure: go to any La Banque Postale branch, explain the situation, and they are legally obliged to open an account for you within 3 business days.

How long does it take?

  • Online banks: 5–10 days to receive your card by post
  • Traditional banks: appointment in 1–2 weeks, card arrives 2 weeks after that
  • La Banque Postale (droit au compte): 3 business days to activate the account
💡 Tip: Ask your employer if they can delay your first salary payment by a week — most HR departments in Strasbourg's international organisations are used to this situation.

What about Wise or Revolut?

Many newcomers use Wise or Revolut as a temporary solution while waiting for a French bank account. They work well for day-to-day spending, but French employers often won't accept them for salary payments, and CAF requires a proper French IBAN. Use them as a bridge, not a long-term solution.
