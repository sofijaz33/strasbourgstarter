Admin & Banking in Strasbourg

Phone needs address. Address needs bank. Bank needs phone. Welcome to the catch-22 that greets every newcomer to France. This guide walks you through exactly how to break the cycle — in the right order, without losing your mind.

Congratulations — you've made it to Strasbourg. The cathedral is breathtaking, the tarte flambée is better than you imagined, and you now need to open a bank account. Simple, right?

Wrong. Here's how the loop goes: the rental agency won't give you a flat without a French phone number so they can contact you. The phone operator won't give you a contract without a French address. The bank won't open an account without proof of address. And you can't easily get proof of address without a flat. Meanwhile you're sitting in an Airbnb watching your savings evaporate at €90 a night wondering why you didn't just stay home.

⚡ Read this before anything else

The secret is to tackle things in a very specific order, using temporary solutions that unlock the next step — rather than trying to get everything perfectly set up at once. Think of it like a video game: you need the small key before you can open the big door.

Also: ask a colleague or friend if you can temporarily register at their address. It sounds awkward, but every expat who has been in Strasbourg for more than six months has done exactly this for someone else. Ask them to put your name on their mailbox — yes, literally — because your bank card, social security number, and other official documents will arrive by post to that address.

Step 1 — Get a French phone number on Day 1

Step 01

Buy a prepaid SIM card — no address needed

Your very first task, ideally before you have even unpacked your suitcase, is to get a French phone number. Not a contract — just a prepaid SIM (carte SIM prépayée). You can buy one at:

  • Any tabac (tobacconist — look for the red diamond sign on every other street corner)
  • Carrefour, Leclerc, or Auchan supermarkets
  • The Gare de Strasbourg (train station) — several phone shops inside
  • Orange, SFR, or Bouygues stores in the city centre

Lebara, Lycamobile, or La Poste Mobile are your best options — cheap (from €5–10), no contract, no French bank account required, and you'll have a real French +33 number within minutes. You'll need your passport or ID card to register. That's all.

This number goes on every single form you fill in for the next three months. Guard it accordingly.

Reality check: Yes, you'll be on a prepaid SIM with 5GB of data when you're used to unlimited everything. Yes, it will feel like 2009. Yes, you will survive. Upgrade to a proper plan — Free Mobile at €8–12/month is excellent value — once you have a French bank account. One battle at a time.

Step 2 — Sort a temporary address

Step 02

Use a friend, colleague, or your employer's HR team

If you don't yet have your own flat, you need a French address for documents. Here are your options in order of ease:

  • Ask a colleague or friend if you can use their address temporarily. Tell them you'll need your name on their mailbox — important, because bank cards and official letters are sent by post and require your name to be physically on it. Most people say yes without hesitation.
  • Ask your employer's HR team for an attestation d'hébergement — a letter confirming you are being hosted at a French address. Many international employers in Strasbourg, especially those linked to EU institutions, are very familiar with this situation.
  • Your Airbnb or temporary accommodation address works as a last resort for online bank sign-ups — some accept it for the first 30 days while your file is processed.
⚠️ One thing you need to know about living in France: Every first Wednesday of the month, sirens sound across the entire country at noon as a national civil alert test (Des sirènes retentissent partout en France chaque premier mercredi du mois). It lasts about 90 seconds. Do not panic. Do not look for shelter. Just nod knowingly, because you live here now. We mention this because the first time it happens, you will be standing in a post office collecting your bank card and you will absolutely think something has gone catastrophically wrong.

Step 3 — Open Wise or Revolut immediately

Step 03

Your financial lifeline while everything else gets sorted

Wise and Revolut are not French banks, but both give you a working Euro account with a French IBAN (starting with FR76) within 24–48 hours. You can sign up entirely on your phone using your home country address if needed.

  • Wise — the most reliable for receiving international transfers. Excellent exchange rates and genuinely transparent fees. wise.com ↗
  • Revolut — fastest to set up, has a French branch, IBAN works for most French services. revolut.com ↗

Important caveat: some French employers and the CAF housing benefit office will not accept a Wise or Revolut IBAN for salary or benefit payments. Use these as a bridge, not a permanent solution — but they will absolutely keep you going while you sort the real thing.

Step 4 — Get a real French IBAN with Nickel or N26

Step 04

The easiest genuine French bank account to open

Nickel is your secret weapon. It is a payment account available at most tabac shops across Strasbourg. Walk in, show your passport, pay €20/year, and walk out with a real French RIB (relevé d'identité bancaire) and a Mastercard. No proof of address. No credit check. No appointment. No waiting.

A French RIB is the document that unlocks almost everything: rental contracts, salary payments from your employer, CAF registration. Once you have a RIB, the chicken-and-egg problem is officially solved.

N26 is the fully digital alternative — open on your phone in about 10 minutes with just your passport and a selfie video. Gives you a French IBAN accepted by most employers.

  • Nickel — walk into any tabac, €20/year, instant RIB ✅
  • N26 — fully online, free basic plan, French IBAN ✅
  • Bunq — another option, no French address required, works for EU residents ✅
Honest moment: The Nickel account has its limitations — no cheque book, no overdraft, and your card will occasionally fail at unmanned motorway toll booths on the road to Colmar. But at this stage of your Strasbourg life, you will not care even slightly. You will have a French RIB and you will feel like you have personally defeated the entire French administrative system. You have not — but enjoy the feeling while it lasts.

Step 5 — Open a proper French bank account

Step 05

Once you have an address — go online first

Now that you have a flat, a French number, a temporary RIB, and proof of address, you can open a full French bank account. The best options for newcomers to Strasbourg:

  • Boursorama Banque — France's largest online bank. No monthly fees, excellent app, accepts foreigners without drama. Apply online, card arrives in about a week. The gold standard for expats. boursorama.com ↗
  • Hello Bank (BNP Paribas digital) — Also free, solid app, backed by a major French bank if you ever need a physical branch.
  • Crédit Mutuel — The most foreigner-friendly traditional bank specifically in Alsace. Some Strasbourg branches have English-speaking staff. Good if you want face-to-face support.
  • La Banque Postale — Available at any post office. Slower and a bit clunky, but legally required to accept you (see below).

Documents you will typically need:

  • Valid passport or EU ID card
  • Proof of French address (rental contract, EDF electricity bill, or landlord's attestation)
  • Proof of income or situation (work contract, payslip, student enrolment, or self-employment certificate)
  • Your existing French RIB from Nickel or N26
  • For non-EU citizens: your visa or residence permit (titre de séjour)

What if a bank refuses you? The droit au compte

French law gives every legal resident the right to a basic bank account — this is called the droit au compte. If a bank turns you down, here is exactly what to do:

  1. Ask for a written refusal from the bank — they are legally required to provide one
  2. Go to the Banque de France website (banque-france.fr ↗) or visit a branch and submit a droit au compte request
  3. The Banque de France designates a bank — usually La Banque Postale — to open an account for you within 3 business days

It is not glamorous and you will not get a cheque book, but you will have a working French bank account and a RIB. Nobody can legally refuse you at that point.

Will anyone at the bank speak English?

At traditional French bank branches in Strasbourg — probably not, or at least not comfortably. Being so close to the German border, you are actually more likely to find a teller who speaks German than one who speaks fluent English. This is not rudeness. It is just France.

A few practical strategies that actually work:

  • Crédit Mutuel branches in the city centre have the best track record for English-speaking advisors — call ahead before making an appointment
  • Online banks (Boursorama, Hello Bank, N26) handle everything via app and email, so the language barrier largely disappears
  • Bring a French-speaking colleague if you need to visit a branch in person — buy them a coffee after, they deserve it
  • Google Translate's camera mode works surprisingly well for French bank documents in a pinch
  • If you work at an EU institution, ask your HR or onboarding team — they often have contacts and internal guides for exactly this situation
⚡ Final tip

Once you have a stable address and French bank account, set up Boursorama as your main account — it is free, the app is excellent, and it handles everything including CAF payments and salary deposits without any fees. Keep Nickel or N26 as a backup card. Keep Wise for international transfers.

And when someone you know moves to Strasbourg after you? Let them use your address. Put their name on the mailbox. Pay it forward. It is the Strasbourg way.

The honest summary: The French banking system is a bit like the tram network in Strasbourg — elegant, reliable, and deeply logical once you understand it, but completely baffling when you first arrive and you're standing at the wrong stop going the wrong direction. Give it three months. You'll be the one explaining the whole thing to the next bewildered newcomer.
