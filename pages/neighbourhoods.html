Neighbourhoods in Strasbourg

Where you live shapes everything — your commute, your neighbours, your school catchment area, and how quickly you feel at home. This guide covers every major quartier honestly, plus a full section on schools for families moving with children.

Strasbourg is a compact city — you can cycle across it in 25 minutes — which means almost every neighbourhood has good access to the centre. The real differences are in atmosphere, price, school catchment, and proximity to the institutions. Here's an honest guide to each.

Quick overview — which neighbourhood for whom

  • Young professional, social life priority → Krutenau or Esplanade
  • Family, good schools, not too far from centre → Conseil des XV or Neudorf
  • Working at EU institutions, want to walk/cycle to work → Orangerie or Conseil des XV
  • Nature, space, quiet, diplomatic vibe → Robertsau
  • Budget-conscious, good tram links → Cronenbourg or Hautepierre
  • Maximum charm, tourist-postcard living → Historic centre or Petite France (but expect high prices and low availability)

Historic centre & Petite France

Historic centre & Petite France

Premium Tourists Low availability

The UNESCO-listed historic centre and the photogenic Petite France district are what most people picture when they think of Strasbourg — half-timbered houses, winding canals, cobblestone streets, and the cathedral looming over everything. It is genuinely beautiful to live in.

That said, it comes with trade-offs. Availability is low, rents are high, parking is essentially non-existent, and on weekends from November to January the Christmas market tourists will be in your living room (metaphorically). If you want the real city experience rather than the tourist brochure, you might be happier a little further out.

A note on Petite France's name: it comes from a 15th century hospital for syphilis patients. The half-timbered houses are genuinely old and genuinely charming. The romantic backstory is entirely fabricated.

Rent (1-bed)€750–1,000/mo
TramLines A, C, D, E, F
Best forCouples, singles

Krutenau

Krutenau (La Krut')

Lively Students Great bars

The Krutenau — always called "la Krut'" by anyone who lives there — is Strasbourg's most energetic neighbourhood. Formerly a fishermen's and tanners' district on the banks of the Ill, it has transformed over the decades into the city's social hub: bars, independent cafés, restaurants, record shops, bookshops, and the University campus nearby.

It is the obvious choice for young professionals and students who want to be in the middle of everything. The Rhine quays are a five-minute walk for evening runs. The centre is walkable. The nightlife is the best in the city. Rents are higher than you'd expect for a student area because everyone wants to live here.

Not ideal for families with young children — the noise and parking situation are challenging, and the school catchment for this area is not particularly strong at primary level.

Rent (1-bed)€650–900/mo
TramLines A, D
Best forYoung professionals, students

Neudorf

Neudorf

Best value Families Local character

Neudorf is Strasbourg's most densely populated neighbourhood and arguably its most underrated. South of the centre, it has a genuinely local character — markets, independent shops, a mix of ages and backgrounds — without the tourist overlay that comes with living in Petite France.

For expats, Neudorf offers the best quality-to-price ratio in the city. Good tram connections (line A runs through it), a growing restaurant scene, the Rivetoile shopping centre, and a real neighbourhood feel. It has transformed significantly since 2022 — new restaurants, improved cycling infrastructure, and rising prices that reflect its growing popularity.

Good for families — reasonable rents, some well-regarded schools in the catchment area, and easy cycling to the centre. Less international than Orangerie or Conseil des XV, which some people find refreshing and others find isolating — depends on what you're looking for.

Rent (1-bed)€600–800/mo
TramLine A
Best forFamilies, value-seekers

Orangerie & European Quarter

Orangerie & European Quarter

Premium Institutions Families

The Orangerie district surrounds Strasbourg's most beautiful park — the Parc de l'Orangerie, with its storks, its lake, its rose gardens, and the occasional flamingo. It sits immediately adjacent to the European Parliament and Council of Europe, which makes it the natural home for institution staff who want to walk or cycle to work in ten minutes.

It is Strasbourg's most upmarket residential area — elegant apartments, wide boulevards, diplomatic residences, excellent schools in the catchment, and prices to match. If you work at an institution and have children, this is where many of your colleagues will live. The international community here is dense, which is either a reassuring cocoon or a bubble depending on your perspective.

The European Quarter itself (immediately around the Parliament and CoE) has less residential character but excellent transport links and all the amenities that come from thousands of institution staff working nearby.

Rent (1-bed)€800–1,100/mo
TramLines E, F
Best forInstitution staff, families

Conseil des XV

Conseil des XV

Families Institutions Hidden gem

Conseil des XV is the neighbourhood that experienced expat families quietly recommend to each other. It sits between the European Quarter and Robertsau — close enough to the institutions to cycle in fifteen minutes, far enough to feel genuinely residential and calm. It has good schools, real streets with real neighbours, and none of the tourist pressure of the historic centre.

The name refers to the old administrative council of fifteen that once governed this part of Strasbourg. Today it is a solidly middle-class residential area with good primary schools in its catchment — which is the main reason institution families with children gravitate here. Rents are slightly lower than the Orangerie while still offering a comfortable, well-connected life.

If you work at the Council of Europe or the European Court of Human Rights and have school-age children, Conseil des XV is probably the most logical neighbourhood to look at first. Most people who end up here stay for years.

Rent (1-bed)€650–850/mo
TramLines E, F (nearby)
Best forInstitution families, long-term residents
⚡ The Conseil des XV insider tip

The primary school in the Conseil des XV catchment has a strong reputation among institution families — good teaching, a genuinely international mix of children, and staff who are experienced with non-francophone newcomers. If you're moving with children and working at the institutions, check this catchment area before signing any lease. The difference in school quality between catchment areas in Strasbourg is real and worth researching before you commit to a flat.

Robertsau

Robertsau

Nature Families Diplomatic

Robertsau is Strasbourg's "green lung" — a leafy, peaceful residential district in the northeast of the city, bordered by the Robertsau forest and the Rhine. It feels more like a village than a city neighbourhood, which is exactly why its residents love it and why others find it too quiet.

Houses with gardens — rare in Strasbourg — are still findable here, though prices have risen. The neighbourhood attracts diplomatic families, institution staff who want space and nature, and anyone who prioritises a calm environment over urban energy. The Parc de Pourtalès is one of Strasbourg's finest green spaces, and the forest is literally on the doorstep.

Downsides: fewer shops and restaurants than other neighbourhoods, and the tram link to the centre (line E) takes longer than cycling from closer-in areas. If you don't have a bike or a car, it can feel slightly isolated.

Rent (1-bed)€700–950/mo
TramLine E
Best forFamilies, nature lovers, diplomatic community

Cronenbourg & Hautepierre

Cronenbourg & Hautepierre

Budget Good tram links

Cronenbourg and Hautepierre are Strasbourg's most affordable neighbourhoods, situated in the northwest of the city. They have historically had a mixed reputation — particularly Hautepierre, which was a designated priority urban zone — but both have undergone significant renovation and investment in recent years.

For newcomers on a tight budget, or for people who simply want more space for their money, these neighbourhoods offer reasonable tram connections and lower rents. They are not the obvious choice for the international community, but they are not the no-go zones they are sometimes unfairly portrayed as either.

Cronenbourg is also home to the famous Caserne de Cronenbourg — where the annual Bal des Pompiers takes place on 13 July. If you live nearby, you will know about it.

Rent (1-bed)€450–650/mo
TramLines B, F
Best forBudget-conscious newcomers

Schools in Strasbourg — the complete guide

School choice in France is not optional — it is determined by your home address. This makes the neighbourhood decision and the school decision inseparable. Before you sign a lease, look up the school catchment area (secteur scolaire) for that address. The Mairie de Strasbourg website has a tool to do this — or simply ask the landlord or agency directly.

How the French school system works

The French system runs in four stages, all free in public schools, compulsory from age 3:

Ages 3–6
École maternelle
Three years (Petite, Moyenne, Grande Section). Play-based but structured. Full school days. Compulsory since 2019.
Ages 6–11
École primaire
Five years (CP to CM2). French, maths, science, history, first foreign language. Homework from CE1. Same teacher all year.
Ages 11–15
Collège
Four years (6ème to 3ème — counting down). Multiple teachers, broader curriculum. Ends with the Brevet exam.
Ages 15–18
Lycée
Three years leading to the Baccalauréat — France's university entrance qualification. General or vocational tracks.
⚠️ Wednesday is different: French schools typically have no classes on Wednesday afternoon (sometimes the whole day for younger children). This is deeply embedded in French family life. Make sure you have childcare sorted for Wednesday afternoons before you arrive — it catches many newcomers off guard.
⚡ For children arriving without French

Ask the school about UPE2A — Unité Pédagogique pour Élèves Allophones Arrivants. This is a dedicated French language support programme for children who don't speak French. Most Strasbourg schools have access to it. Children typically spend part of their week in UPE2A sessions and gradually integrate into mainstream classes as their French improves. It works well — children pick up French remarkably fast when they're immersed.

The international school — ISS

International — IB curriculum
International School Strasbourg (ISS)
The main English-language international school in Strasbourg. Bilingual French-English, IB curriculum from pre-K (age 3) to grade 11 (age 18). Popular with institution families and the wider international community. Before and after school care included in fees (7:45am–6pm). Apply early — popular year groups fill up fast. internationalschoolstrasbourg.com ↗
Fees: €11,800–€16,750/year · Ages 3–18 · IB Diploma
On ISS admissions: Apply the moment you know you're moving to Strasbourg — even before you have a confirmed start date. "We applied six months early and still had to wait" is something you will hear at every institution social event. The school is good. The waiting list is real.

The best public options for expat families

Public lycée — international sections
Lycée International des Pontonniers
Strasbourg's best-kept secret for expat families. A public lycée (so essentially free) with international sections in English, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Polish. Leads to the OIB — Option Internationale du Baccalauréat — which is recognised internationally as a bilingual qualification. Competitive entry, but the combination of quality and zero cost makes it the most compelling option for older children. Ask about admissions at the start of the school year before your child's entry year.
Free (public school) · Ages 15–18 · OIB diploma · International sections
Public — Franco-German bilingual
Lycée Franco-Allemand (LFA) / Collège Vauban
A unique Franco-German school offering a bilingual French-German curriculum leading to the AbiBac — the French Baccalauréat and the German Abitur simultaneously. Exceptional for children with German or who are committed to learning it. Entry requires a German language assessment. Based at the same site as Collège Vauban, which also has strong international sections.
Free (public school) · Ages 11–18 · AbiBac · German required
Private — subsidised Catholic schools
Saint-Étienne, Saint-Jean and others
Strasbourg has several well-regarded private Catholic schools operating under contract with the state — which means they follow the national curriculum and are significantly subsidised (fees are low, typically a few hundred euros per year). Good reputation for structure, smaller classes, and academic results. A solid option if the catchment area school doesn't appeal and you want an alternative without paying ISS fees.
Low fees · Various ages · French national curriculum

Schools and neighbourhoods — the connection

To bring it back to where this guide started: your school catchment area is determined by your address. The neighbourhoods with the strongest school reputations for expat families are:

  • Conseil des XV — the top choice for institution families with children at primary level. Good local school, international mix of children, staff experienced with non-francophone newcomers.
  • Orangerie — excellent catchment schools, proximity to institutions, but higher rents.
  • Robertsau — good schools, calm environment, more space. Slightly further from the centre.
  • Neudorf — improving rapidly, decent schools, best value for families.

For secondary school, catchment matters less — the Lycée des Pontonniers and the LFA both draw from across the city based on admission criteria rather than address. But for primary and maternelle, where you live is where your child goes to school.
